[Hackaday Supercon 2026](https://hackaday.io/superconference/) brought hardware hackers to Pasadena for three days of talks, workshops, and badge hacking.

This was the tenth Supercon, held November 6 to 8 at the ArtCenter Campus. 

The first round of speakers covered display timing hacks, DOOM running on an FPGA, tiny ASIC GPUs, and integrated photonics. 

Tiny Tapeout was one of the event's sponsors.

Pat Deegan led a Tiny Tapeout workshop at the event, and 29 participants took part. The workshop was built around designing and manufacturing a real ASIC. Participants learned the basics of digital logic and how semiconductors are designed and made. 

 {{< figure src="images/hackaday.jpg" title="Tiny Tapeout Workshop at Hackaday Supercon 2026" >}}

They used an online digital design tool to build and simulate a simple design, and they created the GDS files for manufacture on the open-source Sky130 PDK.

 {{< figure src="images/participant.jpg" title="" >}}

Participants also had the option to submit their designs to be manufactured on the next Tiny Tapeout shuttle, [TTSky26d](https://app.tinytapeout.com/shuttles/ttsky26d). The workshop was made possible by sponsorship from [ChipFoundry.io](https://chipfoundry.io/).

Thank you to Hackaday for hosting Supercon, to ChipFoundry for sponsoring the workshop, to the TA's who helped us during the workshop, and to everyone who came to Pasadena to build.
